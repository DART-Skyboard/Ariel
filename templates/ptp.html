The torus is designed just right

Buoyancy in three orders

First is aerospace buoyancy. Outside the hull is vacuum, the root. A container of sea-level air against that root is already buoyant. Because the root reaches the vacuum, it can operate the orders under it.

Second is maritime buoyancy. Fluid against fluid. On the walls that are not making gravity, a pendulum rides the torus contour. Two bobs swing and return against each other, so the energy stays symmetric. The swing keeps the cabin air spread and points the pressure at the gravity wall. It does not pile the air there.

Third is geometric buoyancy. The container’s own volume. On the cubicle the gap between the shells is six habitats — deck, overhead, port, starboard, fore, aft — each sealed from the other five. Which wall is down is an assignment, not a spin. Any habitat can take gravity, return to equalization, or blend if down should meet a corner. The seals stay shut.

Reflex Potentials

Reflex Potentials is the shell around the PTP logic. The Taurus operates inside it. The shell does not replace the chamber, and the chamber does not replace the shell. Each one feeds the other, and each one can operate the other. A change in pressure, gravity, temperature, or gas moves the shells. A change in the shells moves the passive gear, the spread, and the filtration. Gold is geometric mass. Blue is maritime lift. Violet is the aerospace root. The neuron in the median is where those three orders meet. The spines are the links. The bodies are small, transparent, and glowing, and they are collision bodies, so a pressurization change or a new down can throw them into a wall and the wall can throw them back.

Quantity is buoyancy. Raising the count puts more reflex points in the shell. Lowering it takes buoyancy out. Each new body initializes into the pressure that is already there. Tessera projection

The exchange between the shell and the chamber is carried by Tessera Projection. It is its own function, and it initializes itself. It does not wait for a crew to turn it on. Where a reflex body or the cabin gas meets a surface, a virtual brick is born for that contact and only for that contact. A coarser tessellation uses larger bricks. A finer one uses smaller bricks, so the hit can be read more sharply than the chamber mesh. Healthy filtration turns the contact green. A loss of pressure in the shell gap turns it amber and lets the bodies move into that gap.

Tessera is also the catch. A function can already be running — a change of down, a filtration pass, a spring winding down — when something else arrives: a temperature off its set point, a pressure loss, a foreign gas, or a mechanical step that was not the one the habitat was in the middle of. That overlap is a hazard, even when nobody intended it. Tessera is present for it. It marks the contact, and it offers a passive compensation: temperature walks back toward the set point, the pressure trim eases, the wind is given a little of its spring, and the fault is taken down without a powered scrubber and without dumping the gas. The habitat keeps operating. The compensation is the self-driving part. You can watch it. If the shells settle and the bricks go green, the catch held.

What Earth already does

A person on Earth does not feel “buoyant.” What they feel is that the atmosphere lets them live. The connection is bacteria and humidity at a surface: skin, the hide of an animal, the face of a machine. In the right humidity that living fraction is the proportion between the body and the gas. The air presses the surface. The same air is what they breathe. The system is already both mechanical and buoyant.

PTP rebuilds that loop on purpose, and passively. As needs change across the six habitats, the cabin, and the crew, equalization does not ask for more gas and does not throw gas away. The amount stays the amount, unless a crew uses the additional hardware to introduce a measure. Sea-level pressure, 101.3 kilopascals, is the set point shared by the cabin and the habitat that is acting as gravity. The equality is with that side, not with the outer hull. The outer hull is structure against vacuum.
