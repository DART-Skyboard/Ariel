Sets the combustion chamber's pressure and temperature, the ambient atmosphere, the particle count and the thermal/emission scaling, then drives a live exhaust jet from the nozzle: exit velocity from the isentropic nozzle relations, a bell/cone plume that expands or contracts with the ambient pressure, Mach (shock) diamonds spaced by the nozzle exit diameter and the pressure ratio, turbulent edge mixing, and a real ground impingement with a radial wall jet. Drawn in both the point-sprite and the volumetric views. Edit the chamber's pressure/temperature or the atmosphere afterwards and the plume follows. The particle count applies to the baked flow on the next Simulate press.

◈ Particles
Texture-space detail for the floor's heat-splash pattern and per-part thermal shading — independent of any object's own mesh density, so a low-poly import still gets crisp thermal detail. 512 is a solid default; raise it if you want finer detail and your device can spare the GPU memory, lower it if the simulation feels sluggish.
Gaussian splat draws every particle (baked flow and live leaks) as its own soft 3D Gaussian billboard, stretched along its velocity so jets and plumes streak, blended additively with no sorting. It reuses the thermal colour ramp, the collision SDF (gas is faded out where it would sit inside a solid wall) and the X-ray glow, and swells with local pressure so pressurised gas looks denser. One instanced draw call, no density-grid rebuild and no ray-march — lighter than Volumetric, richer than Points.
Replaces the point-sprite particle display with a continuous ray-marched density field — the same rendering approach ArcLake-iOS uses for its fluid view, reusing Arc Flow's existing real-mesh collision SDF so the fluid is shaped by (and never renders inside) the actual imported geometry. The waypoint/physics simulation itself is unchanged — this only swaps the VISUAL layer.
On by default: this adds a per-frame CPU density-texture rebuild and a full-screen ray-march pass on top of the existing simulation, which costs noticeably more than point sprites on an integrated/mobile GPU. Untick it to fall back to point sprites if it runs choppy on your device.
Gas inside a container, channel or combustion chamber is lit in the cavity (hue shift + soft fill). Part shells stay depth-tested and are only made lightly see-through while simulating — they are not light sources. Applies to Points / Volumetric / Gaussian; untick to hide the gas behind opaque walls.
Fixed amount: containers deplete and the flow stops when they are empty. A typed total is shared between the oxidizer (or fuel) containers by volume, and capped at what fits; blank uses each part's own Mass, else the demo fill.
On (default since v74; ?solver=baked turns it off): flow is driven by the pressure differences in a lumped network — helium blowdown and regulator, liquid channels, an accumulating chamber with a choked throat, spill to the atmosphere. The feed lines prime first, then the propellant that reaches the chamber stays inside the cavity until the chamber pressure is high enough to choke the throat, and only then jets out through the neck (this start-up is shown in slow motion, x0.03, then ramps to real time; ?slowmo=1 turns that off). Off: the baked waypoint flow. Applied when Simulate starts. Fluid that crosses into the atmosphere is what the plume and LTCBP evaluate.
Only containers hold fluid, with a finite fill from their volume, density, pressure and temperature; pipes and chambers start empty and only conduct. Continuous Fill (default): containers are replenished as fluid is used, so the steady state can be studied indefinitely. Fixed amount: the amounts deplete, container pressure falls with the mass that leaves, and the flow stops when they are empty. Separated openings spill into the atmosphere; an opening pressed against another surface is plugged.
